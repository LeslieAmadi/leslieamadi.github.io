Helix Arena Tournaments & Promotions

A gamification layer for the Helix remote game server. Operators run time-boxed tournaments, prize drops, and raffles across their whole game portfolio — configured once, scored automatically, settled with an auditable report.

Overview

Helix Arena turns ordinary play into competition. During a tournament, players earn points for real-money play and climb a shared leaderboard; when the clock runs out, the top ranks split a prize pool. Scheduling, eligibility, scoring, and prizes are all configured in the back office — no engineering work per campaign.

The product exists because real-money gaming is undifferentiated at the reel: the same titles appear on dozens of sites. Tournaments give operators a low-cost, time-boxed reason to choose their lobby this weekend.

Scope

This document covers tournaments in depth and sketches two adjacent mechanics — prize drops and raffles — that reuse the same eligibility, scheduling, and settlement spine. Network (cross-operator) tournaments are out of scope for v1.

Goals &amp; non-goals

Goals
  • Launch a compliant tournament in under ten minutes, no developer involvement.
  • Score play automatically and update the leaderboard in near-real-time.
  • Keep promotion cost predictable: a fixed, pre-funded prize pool.
  • Produce an auditable winners report that maps to the operator’s payout process.
  • Work across the full catalogue and all currencies without per-game setup.
Non-goals (v1)
  • Cross-operator / networked prize pools.
  • Scoring on bonus-funded or free play.
  • Automated prize crediting (settlement hands over a report).
  • Event-level scoring (points for specific symbols/features).
  • Recurring/series scheduling.

Audience &amp; roles

Operator

Campaign Manager

Creates and schedules tournaments, sets prizes and eligibility, pulls the winners report. Wants speed, guardrails, and predictable cost.

End user

Player

Discovers a tournament in-game, opts in, plays qualifying games, tracks rank. Wants to know how to earn points and how close a prize is.

Internal

Account & Support

Helps plan promotions and fields rank/prize queries. Needs the leaderboard and report to stay accessible after a tournament ends.

Cancellation forfeits points

An operator may cancel any time before settlement. Players are notified, accrued points are discarded, and no prizes are paid. This is distinct from the natural end, which always settles and pays out.

Ranking models

Each tournament uses exactly one ranking model, chosen at creation. A model is a pure function of a player’s qualifying play. Helix Arena ships six.

How features and bonuses count

SituationScoring rule
Free / feature roundsScored against the paid round that triggered them. Returns add to that cycle; the multiplier denominator stays the triggering stake. The cycle counts as one round for Volume.
Bonus-campaign playFree, operator-funded spins never earn points. Only real-money stakes qualify.
GambleScored on the round that opened it. The final gamble outcome folds into that round’s return; intermediate steps add no separate points.
Below minimum stakeRounds under a configured minimum are ignored entirely — no points, and the player is warned in-game.
Currency

For Stake and Peak Stake, each stake is converted to the tournament’s base currency at the round’s capture-time rate, so one leaderboard stays fair across currencies.

Player experience

The in-game widget is the player’s whole view of a tournament. The flow, kept deliberately light:

  • Discover — a trophy entry point on qualifying games while Scheduled or Live, with a subtle state cue.
  • Join — Join, Later, or Decline. With opt-in off, the player is already in.
  • Compete — live leaderboard with rank, score, nearest competitors, prize positions, time left.
  • Stay informed — notifications for joining, a too-low-stake warning, a last-call, and results.
  • Review — the leaderboard stays available through the archive window.
Min-stake warning

Fires when a player bets below the qualifying threshold during a Live tournament — preventing the “I played but scored nothing” support ticket.

Prizes &amp; settlement

Prizes are modelled as rank ranges, not one row per winner, so a manager can express “ranks 6–20 each win 25” in a single line.

Multi-currency

A tournament is configured in one base currency. Players in other currencies share the same leaderboard and pool: prize values are shown converted, and stake-based scores are normalized into the base currency.

Winner selection &amp; ties

At end the leaderboard freezes and ranks resolve top-down. Ties break by who reached the score first — deterministic, explainable, and it avoids splitting indivisible prizes.

Settlement flow

  • Freeze — no further scoring after end time.
  • Rank & break ties — final ordered standings.
  • Generate winners report — player id, rank, score, prize, per brand.
  • Operator credits — pays winners through their own wallet within their SLA, confirms back.
  • Archive — after the visibility window, the record goes read-only.
Why stop at a report

Operators own the player wallet and their AML/KYC checks, so Arena hands them an auditable list rather than crediting funds directly. Automated crediting is a future enhancement.

Adjacent mechanics

Tournaments are one expression of a shared promotions spine — eligibility, scheduling, an in-game widget, and settlement. Two more reuse it:

Prize Drop

Instead of a leaderboard, prizes are awarded at random to qualifying rounds across the window. The pool is seeded up front; a drop mechanic distributes prizes over time, weighting the largest toward the final stretch. Config differs in two ways: no ranking model, and a per-player prize cap.

Raffle

Tickets are granted for reaching qualifying thresholds (e.g. one per unit wagered). A draw at the end allocates the pool across winning tickets — effort raises the odds rather than guaranteeing rank.

Success metrics

Opt-in rate

Share of eligible players who join, where opt-in is on.

Participation depth

Median qualifying rounds per participant.

Incremental GGR

Lift in gross gaming revenue on qualifying games vs. baseline.

Retention lift

Next-day / next-week return rate vs. non-participants.

Prize-cost ratio

Prize pool as a share of incremental GGR.

Time-to-launch

Median minutes from Draft to Scheduled.

Open questions &amp; future

  • Network tournaments — pooling players/prizes across operators raises authorization, currency, and reporting questions.
  • Event-based scoring — points for in-game events would need a reliable game-event feed.
  • Recurring series — “every Friday” scheduling with carried-over branding.
  • Instant settlement — auto-crediting winners requires wallet write-access and shared AML/KYC responsibility.
  • Points weighting — should currency-based models round per round or on the running total?
